About this application

This tool is designed for calculating cell numbers, cell concentrations and suspension volumes for various culture vessels (ibidi µ-Slides, well plates, individual formats and EM grids).

The calculations are approximations intended to support experimental planning and preparation.

Cell splitting protocol

Using the 3T3 cell line as an example, this protocol explains how to maintain a cell culture. Every 3–4 days, the flask with the growing cell culture needs to be passaged, as the cells multiply so quickly that the entire surface becomes covered. Since the cells continue to divide, nutrients become depleted, the culture deteriorates and eventually dies. For this reason, a portion of the cell culture is transferred to a new flask every 3 days, where the cells can multiply again.

Before the cells can be processed, the laminar flow hood must be disinfected and switched on 15 minutes beforehand.
The following liquids must be warmed to 37 °C in a water bath:

  • Cell culture medium
  • PBS
  • EDTA and trypsin (separately or as a mixture)

Prepare a new cell culture flask and label it accordingly.
Also make sure to have enough pipettes ready at hand. The following quantities should be available at minimum:

  • 2 × 5 ml
  • 1 × 2 ml
  • 2–3 × 1 ml or 1000 µl

Cell counting protocol
  1. Mix the cell suspension thoroughly and fill the Neubauer chamber with approximately 10–20 µl of suspension.
  2. Wait a few seconds until the cells are evenly distributed and no longer flowing.
  3. Select the four corner squares under the microscope.
  4. Count all cells within the selected four corner squares.
  5. To avoid double counting, apply the boundary rule: cells on the upper and left boundaries are counted, while cells on the lower and right boundaries are not counted. The middle of the triple boundary lines is decisive here.
  6. The average cell count per large square can then be used to calculate the cell concentration of the suspension.

Tip: If significantly fewer than 20 or more than around 100 cells per large square are present, the sample should be concentrated or diluted accordingly and counted again.

Remember:
✅ Count the upper line
✅ Count the left line
 ❌ Do not count the lower line
❌ Do not count the right line

Neubauer counting chamber

The Neubauer Improved counting chamber has a defined chamber depth of 0.1 mm. This allows the cell concentration of the suspension to be determined directly from the counted cells.

The chamber is filled by capillary action. Usually, a small drop of about 10–20 µl of cell suspension is sufficient to completely fill the measuring area.

The suspension should be mixed homogeneously before application to prevent sedimentation of the cells.

Which seeding density should I use?

The optimal cell density depends strongly on the cell type and experimental design. Adherent cell lines are often seeded at 50 to 500 cells/mm².

The values calculated here serve as a starting point and should be optimized experimentally.

The following in-house empirical values for 3T3 fibroblasts have proven reliable in micropatterning experiments on 18-well ibidi µ-Slides (cultivation for 24 hours in the incubator each time):

  • Single-cell experiments: 90 cells/mm²
  • Investigation of cell clusters: 200 cells/mm²
  • EM grid seeding (dipping method): 10,000 target cells

These values are not universally applicable but refer to the conditions mentioned above. For other cell lines, vessels or cultivation times, they must be adjusted individually.

Details of the calculation model

This tool uses a simplified geometric model. All results are approximations for experimental planning and do not replace empirical calibration.

Cell concentration of the suspension:
From the cells counted in the four corner squares of the Neubauer chamber:
Cell concentration = (cell count / 4) × 10⁴ cells/ml
(with 0.1 mm chamber depth and 1 mm² per large square)

Seeding density:
The target cell number per well divided by the well area gives the seeding density in cells/mm².

Well area:
Rectangular wells: area = width × length
Circular wells: area = π × (diameter/2)²
The area is recalculated automatically whenever the geometry changes.

Volume:
The filling volume is calculated as area × filling height. For the EM grid, no cylindrical well is assumed; instead, a freely standing drop in the shape of a hemisphere is used:
V = 2/3 × π × r³
Here, r is a model assumption (by default half the grid width). The actual drop shape depends on surface tension, contact angle and grid coating. The EM grid option should therefore be understood as a rough estimate.

Units:
Lengths in mm, areas in mm², volumes in µl and ml, cell concentration in cells/mm² or cells/ml.

Limitations of the model:
Actual cell adhesion, pipetting losses, sedimentation, well edge effects, evaporation and the concave meniscus in small wells (especially 18W and 32W) are not taken into account. The calculated volumes may therefore deviate by a few percent. For critical applications, a one-time gravimetric calibration is recommended.
